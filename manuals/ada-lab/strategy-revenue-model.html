Revenue Model & Unit Economics

All numbers are placeholders / illustrative — replace with sourced, modelled figures. [VERIFY] market pricing and effort before relying on any of this.

Revenue streams#

  1. CASA assessments (core) — AL1 and AL2, priced by assurance level + app complexity. Annually revalidated → renewing.
  2. MASA assessments — mobile, per app, periodically revalidated.
  3. DASA — lighter-touch, higher-volume, lower price.
  4. Cloud Config — usually bundled with CASA AL2 to raise average deal value.
  5. Readiness / pre-assessment advisory — structurally separated from the certifying lab to preserve impartiality (sold by a distinct team/entity; see the independence rule below).
  6. Renewals — the compounding annuity; model net revenue retention explicitly.

The independence constraint (shapes everything)#

ISO/IEC 17025 impartiality rules mean the lab cannot certify an application it also remediated/consulted on. Therefore:

  • Keep "get-ready" advisory" and "validate" as separate offerings / teams with a documented firewall, or refer remediation to partners.
  • This is a feature, not just a limit: it creates a clean partner channel (consultants send us the validation; we send them remediation) — see partnership-strategy.md.

Unit-economics model (template)#

Per assessment:

Price (P)              = f(profile, assurance level, app complexity)        [VERIFY market]
Direct cost (C)        = assessor_days × loaded_day_rate
                         + tooling/scan cost
                         + QA/report review
                         + TrustCB/scheme per-assessment fee (if any)       [VERIFY]
Gross margin           = (P − C) / P

Levers that move margin:

  • Automation of ASVS/MASVS evidence collection → fewer assessor days per job (the single biggest lever; see ../delivery/toolchain-and-automation.md).
  • Reuse across profiles (shared evidence pipeline, QMS, assessor pool).
  • Renewal efficiency — year-2 revalidation of an unchanged app is far cheaper to deliver than a first assessment → renewals carry higher margin than new logos.

Capacity model (the real near-term ceiling)#

Because authorized-lab supply is scarce, capacity, not demand, caps near-term revenue:

Annual capacity ≈ (FTE assessors × billable_days/yr) ÷ assessor_days_per_assessment
Annual revenue  ≈ capacity × utilization × avg_price × (1 + renewal_stacking)

Plan hires against a capacity target, not a demand target. Automation raises capacity without linear headcount — the scalability story for investors.

Pricing strategy#

  • Enter at competitive AL1 price to win reference logos and renewals.
  • Make margin on AL2 + Cloud Config bundles (depth incumbents under-serve).
  • Price renewals to retain (lower effort, protect the annuity) rather than to maximize first-year take.
