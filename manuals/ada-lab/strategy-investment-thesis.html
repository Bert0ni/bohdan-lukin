Investment Thesis — ADA Authorized Lab

A fundable new direction for a security company. All figures illustrative — [VERIFY] and replace with modelled numbers before any investor conversation.

The thesis in one line#

Own a scarce, regulator-like license (ADA Authorized Lab) that turns a Google-mandated, annually-recurring compliance requirement into a high-retention service annuity — built on top of penetration-testing capability we already have.

Why this is attractive to an investor#

  1. Mandated, non-discretionary demand. CASA is required by Google for apps using sensitive OAuth scopes and for key marketplace surfaces. Buyers don't choose whether to comply — only who validates them. Demand is structural, not sentiment-driven.
  2. Recurring revenue by design. Validations are revalidated annually → a validated client is a renewing client. Net revenue retention is inherently high once the base exists.
  3. Scarce supply / high barrier to entry. Only ~10 authorized labs globally. The barrier (ISO/IEC 17025 + TrustCB proficiency) is exactly what makes the position defensible — the same wall we must climb keeps future competitors out. This is a moat, not just a cost.
  4. Capital-light, high-gross-margin services. The scarce asset is authorization + trained assessors + automation, not capex. Marginal cost per assessment falls as tooling matures.
  5. Capability adjacency / low execution risk. We already run web/API, mobile and cloud pentests. ADA profiles (CASA/MASA/Cloud Config) are productized, standardized versions of work we do — so the ramp is re-packaging + accreditation, not R&D from zero.
  6. Platform for expansion. One accreditation + lab machinery unlocks four profiles and a credible path to adjacent attestations (SOC 2 readiness, ISO 27001 mapping, Play/Workspace trust services) — land-and-expand within each client.

The "why now"#

  • Google has tightened and routinized CASA for the OAuth ecosystem; the requirement is maturing from niche to standard (see market-opportunity.md).
  • The scheme recently moved to the Linux Foundation and to the TrustCB/ISO-17025 lab model, so the authorized-lab roster is still small and forming — early entrants lock in brand and reference clients.
  • OWASP ASVS 5.0 and MASVS refreshes create a re-mapping cycle that favors labs with modern, automated methodology over incumbents on legacy processes.

What we are asking for#

  • Setup capital to fund: ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation (consultant + body fees), the TrustCB petition and trial evaluations, a small senior assessor + quality team, and an evidence/ automation pipeline — through to first authorized profile (CASA) and initial revenue.
  • Runway to ~Year-2 operating break-even on a modest assessment volume.
  • Indicative sizing and the P&L sketch: ../program-plan/budget-and-financials.md.

Returns logic (how investors win)#

  • Annuity base compounds: each cohort of validated clients renews annually and new cohorts stack on top → revenue grows faster than cost once the lab is authorized.
  • Operating leverage: fixed cost of authorization + QMS is spread over rising volume; automation drops cost/assessment over time.
  • Strategic exit optionality: authorized-lab status is acquirable scarcity. Larger security/assurance firms (the kind already on the roster) buy capability and licenses they can't quickly build — a natural M&A exit (see partnership-strategy.md).

The bet, plainly#

Spend a defined, mostly one-time sum to cross a regulatory-grade barrier that only ~10 firms worldwide have crossed, and sit on the renewing side of a Google-mandated compliance flow. The downside is a capped setup cost; the upside is a scarce, compounding, acquirable annuity.
