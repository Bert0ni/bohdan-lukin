Market Opportunity

All quantities are illustrative frameworks to be populated with sourced data before use — [VERIFY] every number with primary research.

Demand drivers#

  1. Google OAuth / restricted-scope apps. Any third-party app requesting sensitive or restricted Google API scopes (Gmail, Drive, Calendar, Workspace, etc.) must complete CASA — and re-complete it annually. The population of such apps is large and growing (SaaS, AI agents and LLM tools touching mailboxes/drives, integrations, add-ons).
  2. Google Play "Independent security review" (MASA). Mobile developers seek the Play Data- safety badge → recurring MASA demand.
  3. Google Workspace Marketplace. Listing/badging has been tied to higher CASA assurance (AL2) → pulls apps up the assurance ladder (higher-value engagements).
  4. AI-agent explosion. The wave of AI assistants/agents requesting mailbox, document and drive scopes is a net-new, fast-growing cohort of CASA-obligated apps — a timing tailwind.
  5. Procurement spillover. CASA/MASA validation increasingly appears in enterprise vendor- security questionnaires as a trust signal beyond the strict Google mandate.

Sizing framework (TAM / SAM / SOM)#

Build the model top-down and bottom-up and reconcile:

  • TAM — all apps globally obligated to CASA/MASA annually × average assessment price. Input needed: count of restricted-scope OAuth apps + Play apps pursuing the badge. [VERIFY]
  • SAM — the slice we can serve given language, geography, pricing tier and profile scope (CASA + MASA first).
  • SOM (3-yr) — realistic capture given ~10 competitors and our assessor capacity. Capacity is the real near-term ceiling (see revenue-model.md), so SOM is supply-constrained, which is favorable: we can sell out capacity before price competition bites.

Because supply (authorized labs) is the scarce side, the near-term question is "how many assessments can we staff?" more than "can we find demand?". Model capacity first.

Pricing context [VERIFY]#

Public/vendor sources put third-party CASA engagements in a broad range depending on assurance level and app complexity (self-serve/AL1 at the low end; comprehensive AL2 with infra + data-storage review materially higher). Confirm current market pricing from competitor quotes and ADA materials; do not anchor the model on blog figures.

Why a new entrant can win now#

  • Roster still forming post-Linux-Foundation move → room for new authorized labs.
  • Modern, automation-first delivery vs. incumbents' manual processes → better margin and turnaround (turnaround time is a real buyer pain in this market).
  • Multilingual / regional positioning (e.g. CEE/EU, EN+UA+local) can capture underserved segments and regional procurement preferences.
  • Pentest heritage lets us credibly offer AL2 (the deep, infra-inclusive level) where lighter competitors cluster at AL1.

Segments to target first#

  1. AI/LLM SaaS requesting Google scopes (fast-growing, security-aware, willing to pay).
  2. Mid-market SaaS with Workspace Marketplace ambitions (need AL2 → higher value).
  3. Mobile-first products chasing the Play badge (MASA) — bundle with CASA for web+mobile apps.
  4. Agencies / platforms integrating many sub-apps (channel/volume — see partnership-strategy.md).
