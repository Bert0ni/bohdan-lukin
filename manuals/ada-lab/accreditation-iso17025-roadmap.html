ISO/IEC 17025 Accreditation Roadmap

The gating prerequisite. Start on day one — it is the critical path to authorization.

Why ISO/IEC 17025#

ADA/TrustCB require every authorized lab to be ISO/IEC 17025-accredited. ISO/IEC 17025 is the international standard for the competence of testing and calibration laboratories. It proves, through an independent accreditation body, that the lab produces technically valid results under a controlled management system with demonstrated impartiality and competence.

For a security-testing lab, accreditation is granted for a defined scope — the specific test methods (here: the ADA profile methodologies) we are competent to perform.

Choosing the accreditation body#

Accreditation is issued by a national/regional accreditation body that is a signatory to the ILAC MRA (International Laboratory Accreditation Cooperation Mutual Recognition Arrangement), so the result is internationally recognized. Examples by region (choose per our legal domicile):

  • EU/EEA: the national accreditation body (one per country under Regulation 765/2008) — e.g. SNAS (Slovakia), DAkkS (Germany), etc.
  • UK: UKAS. US: A2LA, ANAB. Others per ILAC.

[VERIFY] Confirm with TrustCB whether they require a specific accreditation body or accept any ILAC-MRA signatory, and whether a particular scope wording is expected for ADA profiles. Also confirm domicile — this drives which body applies.

What accreditation requires (the work)#

  1. Define the scope — the exact test methods (CASA/ASVS method; later MASA/MASTG, etc.).
  2. Build the Quality Management System (QMS) covering ISO/IEC 17025 clauses, including:
    • Impartiality & confidentiality (structural independence, conflict-of-interest controls).
    • Personnel competence — training, authorization, and competency records per assessor.
    • Document & record control; control of the assessment process.
    • Method validation / verification — evidence that our CASA method reliably produces valid results.
    • Handling of test items, equipment/tooling control, and measurement/decision traceability (how pass/fail decisions are made and recorded).
    • Nonconforming work, corrective action, complaints & appeals.
    • Internal audits and management review.
  3. Operate the system long enough to generate records (you cannot be audited on an empty QMS).
  4. Internal audit + management review — at least one full cycle before the external audit.
  5. External assessment by the accreditation body — document review + on-site/remote witnessed assessment of real evaluations; close any findings (nonconformities).
  6. Accreditation granted for the scope; thereafter surveillance (periodic re-assessment) and scope extensions when we add profiles.

Indicative timeline [VERIFY]#

From a zero start, a realistic range is ~9–18 months to first accreditation, driven by how fast the QMS is built and how much operating history is required before the assessor audit. Running method-build and dry-runs in parallel compresses this.

Indicative cost drivers [VERIFY]#

  • ISO consultant (strongly recommended for a first-time lab) — the biggest controllable cost.
  • Accreditation body fees — application + assessor days + annual surveillance.
  • Internal effort — a dedicated Quality Manager is effectively required.
  • Tooling/record systems — document control, evidence management.

Quantified ranges live in ../program-plan/budget-and-financials.md.

Parallelization with the ADA track#

  • Build the CASA method as a controlled method inside the QMS, so the same artifact serves the accreditation and the TrustCB trial evaluation.
  • Use dry-run assessments as both QMS operating records and trial-evaluation rehearsal.
  • Schedule the assessor witnessed audit and the TrustCB trial evaluation close together to reuse the same live engagements where allowed.
