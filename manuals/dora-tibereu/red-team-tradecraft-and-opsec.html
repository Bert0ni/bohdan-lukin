Tradecraft & OPSEC

Original guide to red-team operational tradecraft and operations security, with the TIBER safety overlay that governs a regulated TLPT. Tradecraft here means operating like a real adversary while staying safe, controlled and deconflicted.

Operating picture#

flowchart LR OP["Operators"] --> RD["Redirectors"] RD --> C2["C2 servers"] OP --> PAY["Payloads and tooling"] C2 -. telemetry .-> LOG["Oplog and evidence"] OP --> DECON{"Deconfliction with Control Team"} DECON -->|risky or blocked| CT["CT / TM approval · leg-up"]

Infrastructure#

  • Separation &amp; redirection — operators never touch targets directly; traffic flows through redirectors to C2 so infrastructure is resilient and attributable only to the engagement.
  • Attribution hygiene — categorised domains, aged infrastructure, TLS, realistic user-agents; avoid patterns that trivially burn the operation on day one.
  • Segmented tooling — distinct infra per scenario where noise/sophistication differs, so one burnt channel doesn't collapse the whole test.
  • Channel diversity — primary + fallback C2 (e.g. HTTPS, DoH, long-haul) so a single detection doesn't end access prematurely.

OPSEC principles#

  • Least noise for the goal — match tradecraft to the emulated actor; don't be louder than the profile unless a scenario intends it.
  • Know your indicators — understand what each tool/action emits (process, network, auth, file); prefer living-off-the-land where the profile fits.
  • Evidence as you go — timestamped capture of each action (see oplog); OPSEC includes being able to prove what you did and did not do.
  • Data minimisation — collect only what proves the flag; use test tokens/canaries, never real customer data; handle any sensitive artefact per the agreed data rules.

The TIBER safety overlay (what makes a TLPT different)#

flowchart TB ACT["Proposed action"] --> CHK{"In RTTP and RoE? Within risk appetite?"} CHK -->|yes| GO["Execute and evidence"] CHK -->|needs sign-off| APR["CT / TM approval"] CHK -->|unsafe or blocked| STOP["Stop · deconflict · leg-up"] APR --> GO STOP --> GO
  • Blue team stays unaware — nothing should reveal the test; coordination is with the Control Team, not the defenders.
  • Production-safe — no action outside the RoE / RTTP; destructive impact is demonstrated, not caused.
  • Deconfliction — a standing channel to the CT to confirm "was that us?" and to pause on real incidents or unexpected risk.
  • Leg-ups — when a path is blocked and continuation isn't safely/meaningfully possible, request a documented leg-up (pre-agreed triggers/deadlines) so later defences are still tested.
  • Stop conditions — defined up front; the CTL can suspend the test; limited purple teaming may be used to continue safely. See safety &amp; risk.

Evasion — principle, not recipe#

Emulate the detection-evasion sophistication of the profiled actor (e.g. AMSI/ETW-aware, LOLBins, egress blending) to test whether controls detect that class of behaviour — the aim is to measure the defence, not to "win". Tie every evasive choice back to the threat profile and the learning goal, and record it for the Replay/Purple discussion.
