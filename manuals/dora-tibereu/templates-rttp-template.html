Red Team Test Plan (RTTP) — TEMPLATE

Basis: ECB TIBER-EU Red Team Test Plan Guidance, January 2025 (PDF), §2 "Required content" + §3 considerations, mirroring the binding DORA TLPT RTS, Annex IV (Reg. (EU) 2025/1190, full text). Owner: RT / RTTM. Approved by CT + TM before any active attack. Copy per engagement; do not edit this blank. Built from the approved SSD + TTIR + selected scenarios.

Part A — ECB §2 compliance spine (the RTTP shall include ALL of these)#

Prove completeness here; the drafting sections in Part B carry the detail.

  • A1 — Communication channels and procedures to be used.
  • A2 — TTPs allowed and not-allowed in the attack, including ethical boundaries for social engineering and how the privacy of involved parties is safeguarded.
  • A3 — Risk management measures to be followed by the RTT.
  • A4 — A description for each scenario, including: the simulated threat actor · their intent, motivation and goals · the target function(s) and supporting ICT system(s) · the targeted confidentiality, integrity, availability and authenticity aspects · the flags.
  • A5 — A detailed description of each expected attack path, including pre-requisites and possible leg-ups to be provided by the CT, with deadlines for their provision and potential usage.
  • A6 — Scheduling of red teaming activities, with time planning per scenario, at minimum split into the three tester phases: (i) in — entering the entity's ICT systems · (ii) through — moving through them and executing actions on objectives · (iii) out — extracting from them.
  • A7 — Particularities of the entity's infrastructure to be considered during testing.
  • A8 — Any additional information or resources the testers need to execute the scenarios.

Red team composition (§3.2): disclose the full RTT composition, each member's experience, roles and contact details; appoint a red team manager + deputy (the manager is the single point of contact for CT, TM and TIP); document that competencies match the scope. Any change to RTT composition must be communicated to the TM and CTL and captured in a revised RTTP.

Part B — Drafting layout#

1. Document control#

Version · classification · author (RTTM) · reviewers · CT approval · TM approval · distribution · change history.

2. Objectives#

CIFs · attack objectives · flags (from SSD) · success criteria (vs objectives/flags and detection/response — not Domain Admin).

3. Threat scenarios#

Selected scenarios from the TTIR with rationale.

4. Attack paths#

Per scenario: technical path(s) to the flag, prerequisites, decision points, alternatives.

5. TTP mapping#

MITRE ATT&CK mapping and adversary tradecraft per path.

6. Infrastructure & tooling#

Red team infrastructure, C2, tooling, communications, data handling.

7. Execution schedule#

Phases, sequence, parallelism, resources, milestones, dependencies.

8. Risk management#

Production safety, stop conditions, mitigations, risk register linkage. (See Safety & risk management.)

9. Rules of Engagement#

Reference/attach the RoE: permitted/prohibited actions.

10. Communications#

CT/TM escalation paths, deconfliction channel, regular status cadence.

11. Evidence#

Logging, timestamps, proof-of-objective approach, chain of custody. (See evidence register.)

12. Contingencies#

Alternative paths, leg-ups (approval + documentation requirements), recovery/cleanup procedures.

13. Approvals#

CT approval · TM approval · date/version. Active testing starts only after both.
