Targeted Threat Intelligence Report (TTIR) — TEMPLATE

Basis: ECB TTIR Guidance, January 2025 (../assets/source/ecb-ttir-guidance-2025.pdf), §2 "Required content", mirroring the binding DORA TLPT RTS, Annex III (Reg. (EU) 2025/1190, ../assets/source/Delegated-Regulation-EU-2025-1190-DORA-TLPT-RTS.md) — the non-threat-led Scenario X is the one referred to in Art. 10(4). Owner: TIP. Assessed by CT and TM. Classification: fill in. Copy per engagement; do not edit this blank. Written during the TI phase, finalised after scenario selection; at the end, re-validate the SSD flags with the entity.

Part A — ECB §2 compliance spine (the TTIR shall include ALL of these)#

Use this checklist to prove completeness; the report sections in Part B carry the detail.

  • A1 — Overall scope of the intelligence research, including at least: CIFs in scope · their geographical location · official EU language(s) in use · relevant ICT third-party service providers · the period over which research was gathered.
  • A2 — Overall assessment of concrete actionable intelligence (digital footprint), e.g.: employee usernames/passwords · look-alike domains · technical reconnaissance (vulnerable/exploitable software, systems, technologies) · employee social-media info · information for sale on the dark web · other relevant internet/public-network info · where relevant, physical targeting information (access to premises).
  • A3 — Threat intelligence analysis of the general threat landscape and the entity's particular situation, including at least: geopolitical environment · economic environment · technological and other sector-relevant trends.
  • A4 — Threat profiles of the malicious actors (specific or generic class) that may target the entity: systems most likely to be targeted · motivation, intent and rationale · likely modus operandi.
  • A5 — Threat scenarios: at least three end-to-end scenarios for the profiles with the highest threat severity scores, each describing the full end-to-end attack path, with at least one covering availability, one integrity, and one confidentiality.
  • A6 — Scenario X — optionally, one of the three selected scenarios may be a non-threat-led, forward-looking Scenario X (not an additional 4th scenario).

Intelligence gathering may use OSINT and HUMINT with strong ethical conduct, and must not alert the Blue Team. Active reconnaissance by the RTT in the TI phase is allowed only at the TIP's request and after CT + TM approval. Share the TTIR with the entity's CTI team after the test to help them reduce targetable information.

Part B — Report structure (drafting layout)#

Structure informed by the ECB guidance and the Norges Bank example. Each section cites which Part A element(s) it satisfies.

  1. Document Control & Confidentiality — version, classification, author (TIP), reviewers, approvals, distribution, change history.
  2. Executive Summary — key threats, most important findings, recommended scenarios.
  3. Scope & Intelligence Requirements (A1) — CIFs, geography, EU language, ICT third parties, research period; linkage to the SSD.
  4. Business & Critical Functions Overview — business model, dependencies, CIF contextualisation (see Annex 1 business overview input).
  5. Generic Threat Landscape (GTL) (A3) — use the jurisdiction's GTL if one exists; otherwise develop the generic landscape. Geopolitical, economic, technological/sector trends.
  6. Targeted Intelligence & Digital Footprint (A2) — attack surface on a per-CIF basis: people, processes, technologies; leaks, look-alike domains, exposed credentials, dark-web items, physical targeting where relevant.
  7. Threat Actor Assessment & Prioritisation (A4) — longlist → threat severity scoring → shortlist, with rationale and confidence.
  8. Selected Threat Actor Profiles (A4) — motivation, intent, rationale, targeted systems, modus operandi, known campaigns, assessment confidence.
  9. Threat Scenario Longlist — all scenarios considered; selection criteria; reasons for rejection.
  10. Selected End-to-End Threat Scenarios (A5, A6) — ≥3 scenarios; per scenario the end-to-end attack path, MITRE ATT&CK mapping, objective, impact dimension (A/I/C), flag; Scenario X where relevant.
  11. CIF, Scenario & Flag Mapping — scenarios ↔ CIFs ↔ agreed flags.
  12. Intelligence Gaps & Assumptions — limitations, uncertainties, confidence levels.
  13. Scope & Flag Revalidation — proposed SSD flag changes after TI completion.
  14. TI-to-RT Handover — transferred artifacts, RTTM questions, decisions, version history (see handover register).
  15. Appendices & Evidence Register — source references, IOC/TTP tables, OSINT evidence, scoring methodology (see evidence register); Annex 1 — business overview input.

Companion structured data lives in the scenario matrix and evidence register so the TTIR narrative stays readable.

Part C — concise alternative layout (TIBER-NO v1.2)#

For smaller engagements, the TIBER-NO TTIR Guidance v1.2 (04 Feb 2026) offers a lighter "example TTIR structure" — still satisfy every Part A element, this is just a slimmer layout:

  • Executive summary
  • Business overview from an intelligence perspective
  • Intelligence on the entity's digital presence (digital footprint)
  • Threat actors — threat actor assessment → selected threat actor profiling
  • High-level threat scenarios — per scenario: threat level for actor and scenario + attack flow including TTPs
  • Appendices — TTP tables, OSINT raw data, etc.

Governance note (TIBER-NO v1.2): the CT and TCT must agree and attest to the TTIR's contents before the test can progress; the TTIR is then also shared with the RTT. The guidance is not mandatory and may be deviated from — the binding requirements are the ECB TTIR Guidance §2 (Part A) and the DORA RTS.
