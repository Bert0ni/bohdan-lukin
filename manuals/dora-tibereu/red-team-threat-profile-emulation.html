Threat-profile emulation

Original guide to turning a TTIR threat actor into an emulation plan the red team can execute — the bridge between intelligence and operations. It consumes the actor profiles from the TTIR and feeds the RTTP.

From actor to emulation plan#

flowchart LR TA["TTIR actor profile"] --> CAP["Capabilities and intent"] CAP --> TTP["Signature TTPs mapped to ATT&CK"] TTP --> PLAN["Emulation plan: do / don't"] PLAN --> PATH["Attack paths to flags"] PATH --> RTTP["RTTP scenario"]

What a threat profile captures (emulation card)#

FieldContent
Actor / archetypeNamed actor or class being emulated (and the scenario it drives)
Motivation &amp; intentWhy they target this entity / CIF
CapabilitySophistication, resources, custom vs commodity tooling
Signature TTPsConcrete ATT&CK techniques the actor is known/assessed to use
Initial-access preferencePhish, exploit public-facing, supply chain, valid accounts…
Post-exploitation styleDirect-to-objective vs broad presence; stealth vs speed
Tooling to emulateRepresentative C2 / tooling of comparable behaviour (not necessarily theirs)
"Do" listBehaviours in-profile to reproduce
"Don't" listOut-of-profile behaviours to avoid (keeps the test realistic)
Attribution tellsCharacteristic indicators worth reproducing to test detections

Backward- vs forward-looking#

Emulate evidence-based behaviour (what the actor has done) but, as the TTIR allows, add plausible forward-looking TTPs (what they could do next) — keeping the two clearly distinguished, and keeping flexibility for any Scenario X.

Fidelity vs feasibility#

Aim for the highest fidelity that is safe and feasible in the window. Where exact tooling is impractical or unsafe, emulate the behaviour and indicators that exercise the same defences — the goal is to measure detection/response of that TTP class, not to run the actor's actual malware.

Emulation stays inside the RoE and safety &amp; risk rules. Each emulated TTP maps to a flag/CIF and is recorded in the oplog for the Replay/Purple discussion.

Concept credit: adversary-emulation and threat-profile practice as popularised by Joe Vest &amp; James Tubberville — see RedTeam.Guide and their book (further reading in the section index). Text here is original; consult the originals for their treatment.
