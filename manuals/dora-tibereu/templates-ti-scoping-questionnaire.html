TI Scoping Questionnaire & Business-Overview Intake — TEMPLATE

Basis: ECB TTIR Guidance, Jan 2025, Annex 1 "Business overview input" + §3.2 (../assets/source/ecb-ttir-guidance-2025.pdf). This is the structured intake the **entity provides to the TIP at the start of the TI phase, plus the TIP's own direction-setting (intelligence requirements). It feeds the TTIR business-overview and digital-footprint sections and the threat-actor assessment. Owner: completed by the entity (via CT/CTL); structured and analysed by the TIP. Commercially sensitive items: handle pragmatically; keep real data out of this repository**.

Part 1 — Business overview input (entity provides; ECB Annex 1 minimum set)#

  • The entity & its CIFs — explanation of the entity and its critical or important functions and their significance for the broader financial sector.
  • Entity's own threat assessment — including examples of recent adverse cyber events.
  • Systemic implications — potential impact of a compromise of the entity's confidentiality, integrity and availability.
  • Business model & org setup — shareholder ownership, company structure, Board & executive management, products & services, key financial figures.
  • Geographic presence — countries in which the entity operates.
  • Interdependencies — financial and operational; countries from which the entity receives significant supply-chain support (e.g. "IT support outsourced to country X").
  • Client types of potential interest to foreign intelligence (characteristics only — no names).
  • Niche markets the entity is active in.
  • R&D / IP — high-level insight into any niche research knowledge or intellectual property.
  • M&A — high-level insight into possible future mergers/acquisitions that may raise actor interest.
  • Geopolitical exposure — issues or investments that may affect the threat landscape.
  • Third-party involvement in CIFs — details.
  • Technical anchors — the entity's domains and IP addresses.

Rationale (§3.2.2): most of this is available from open source, but entity-provided input lets the TIP concentrate effort on analysis and digital-footprint discovery rather than basic fact-finding.

Part 2 — CIF / systems detail (reconcile with the SSD)#

CIFSub-functionsSupporting key systems/servicesICT third partiesJurisdiction(s)Associated flag(s) + aspect (C/I/Authenticity/A)

This must reconcile with the SSD and the project charter. The TIP does not re-scope — it contextualises what the CT/TM already agreed.

Part 3 — Session logistics & OPSEC#

  • Business-overview session: attendees (CT/CTL, SMEs), date, format, classification.
  • Deconfliction & secrecy: confirm the Blue Team is not informed; agree the codename and need-to-know list; agree encrypted comms / data room (per the project charter).
  • Sensitivity handling: which items are commercially sensitive and how they'll be shared.
  • Points of contact: named CT contacts for follow-up questions during collection.

Part 4 — TIP intelligence requirements (TIP completes — direction/PIRs)#

Translate the SSD CIFs/flags into explicit collection questions (ECB §3.2.4). For each CIF:

  • Target environment: what exposed info would help an actor map the CIF-supporting network, locate CIFs, and find exploitable vulnerabilities? What infra has been targeted before? Which sector-critical vulnerabilities are likely present?
  • Social engineering: which roles/people could be targeted; what contact details/credentials are discoverable?
  • Third parties / MSPs: how do they change the entity's posture; which have been or could be compromised, and how would that enable entity compromise?
