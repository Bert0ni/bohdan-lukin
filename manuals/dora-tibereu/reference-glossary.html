Glossary — definitions & standards

Working definitions for terms that carry specific meaning in TIBER-EU / DORA TLPT. For acronyms see acronyms.md.

Threat-Led Penetration Testing (TLPT)#

A controlled, intelligence-led simulation of real adversary behaviour against a financial entity's critical or important functions. Unlike ordinary penetration testing, scenarios are derived from threat intelligence about real actors and the test assesses not only whether compromise is possible but the organisation's ability to prevent, detect and respond.

TIBER-EU#

The ECB's framework defining how an intelligence-led red-team test is governed, scoped, executed and closed. It is the recognised method EU authorities use to deliver DORA TLPT.

Critical or Important Function (CIF)#

A business function whose disruption would materially impair the financial entity's services, regulatory compliance or soundness. CIFs anchor scope — scenarios and flags map back to them.

Flag#

A pre-agreed, concrete objective proving an attacker reached a defined level of impact on a CIF (e.g. "controlled proof of ability to influence the payment process"). Flags are defined in the SSD and revalidated during TI.

Scope Specification Document (SSD)#

Client-owned document defining CIFs, supporting systems/services/processes, third-party dependencies, flags, boundaries and exclusions. Not just a list of IPs.

Targeted Threat Intelligence Report (TTIR)#

TIP deliverable: generic + targeted threat landscape, digital footprint, prioritised threat actors with profiles, and selected end-to-end threat scenarios mapped to MITRE ATT&CK. ECB 2025 requires ≥3 end-to-end scenarios covering availability, integrity and confidentiality.

Threat scenario#

A high-level narrative: threat actor → objective → entry vector → key TTPs → target CIF → flag → impact dimension. It is attack logic, not yet an approved technical attack plan (that is the RTTP).

Scenario X#

A non-threat-led, forward-looking scenario investigating future or otherwise relevant attack vectors (hybrid, novel TTPs, "out of the box" elements), customised to the entity. Per the TIBER-EU 2025 framework, a maximum of one of the three selected scenarios may be a Scenario X (it counts toward the three, it is not an extra). If none is specified during the TI phase, at most one scenario may be transformed into a Scenario X during active testing, with the agreement of the TM, CT and RTT.

Generic Threat Landscape (GTL)#

A national/sector threat-landscape report that some TIBER-XX jurisdictions produce, covering geopolitical and criminal threats unique to the country. Where a GTL exists, the TIP uses it as foundational input to the TTIR; where none exists, the TIP develops the generic landscape itself.

Threat severity score#

The TIP's prioritisation output ranking threat profiles/actors. Per the ECB TTIR Guidance, the ≥3 end-to-end scenarios are built for the threat profiles with the highest threat severity scores. We implement this as a capability × intent × relevance scoring with documented methodology.

Red Team Test Plan (RTTP)#

RTTM-owned deliverable turning TTIR scenarios into concrete technical attack paths, TTP mapping, infrastructure, execution schedule, risk management, RoE, communications and evidence approach. Active attacks begin only after CT and TM approve the RTTP.

Rules of Engagement (RoE)#

The agreed set of permitted and prohibited actions, escalation paths, testing windows, data-handling rules and emergency-stop procedures governing execution.

Leg-up#

A controlled assist from the CT (access or information) that lets the team validate a later scenario stage when blocked — used to test subsequent defences, not to fabricate reaching a flag. All leg-ups must be approved by CT/TM and documented.

Deconfliction#

The process by which the CT/TM can confirm whether suspicious activity is the red team (so a real incident is never mistaken for the test, and vice versa).

Replay#

A joint walkthrough where RT and BT reconstruct the attack timeline: what the attacker did, what the SOC saw, what was missed, which alerts fired without response.

Purple Teaming (PT)#

A mandatory closure-phase exercise (TIBER-EU 2025, aligned with the DORA RTS) where RT and BT work openly together — reviewing what other steps the RT could have taken and how the BT can improve detection and response. It has its own ECB TIBER-EU Purple Teaming Guidance and must take place within 10 weeks of the end of active testing.

Attestation#

A signed confirmation that the test was conducted in accordance with the core/mandatory requirements of the TIBER-EU framework. In the TIBER-EU 2025 framework it is signed/issued by the TIBER authority (the Test Manager) once it has approved the TSR and Remediation Plan at closure; it provides the grounds for mutual recognition among other relevant authorities. (Note: the legacy 2018 framework had the entity's board and the TI/RT providers sign it — that model is superseded.)

Standards we align to#

  • MITRE ATT&CK — the common language for TTP mapping across TTIR, RTTP and RTTR.
  • ECB TIBER-EU 2025 — lifecycle, roles, timings, deliverables.
  • DORA / Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1190 — binding TLPT requirements.
  • National implementation (TIBER-SK / NBS) — jurisdiction-specific procedure.
