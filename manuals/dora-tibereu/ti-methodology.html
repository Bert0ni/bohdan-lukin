Threat Intelligence Methodology — working as the TIP

How the TI stream runs the threat-intelligence phase (~4–6 weeks) and produces a defensible TTIR document set.

The output is not a raw OSINT dump — it is contextualised reasoning about which attacks are realistic for this entity. Target intelligence (the entity's reality) and threat intelligence (who attacks, and how) alternate and supplement each other, and are never analysed in silos. Recognised CTI tradecraft is used in support of — never instead of — the ECB/RTS requirements.

1 · The engagement as an intelligence cycle

The TI phase runs as a mini intelligence cycle — direction → collection → processing → analysis → dissemination — instantiated as six working steps inside the ~4–6 week window. Supporting lenses: the Diamond Model (adversary–capability–infrastructure–victim) and F3EAD for structuring collection and analysis.

3 · What we collect — the two streams

Target intelligence — the entity's digital footprint (per CIF)

Attack surface of people, processes and technologies, both intentionally published and unintentionally leaked: attack-surface & CT-log mining, subdomain enumeration, tech fingerprinting; shadow IT & exposed cloud/management interfaces; look-alike/typo-squat domains; leaked & breach/infostealer credentials; people footprint (roles, privileged access, contact details); third-party / supply-chain posture; and — where relevant — physical targeting information.

Threat intelligence — who would attack, and how

GTL (use the jurisdiction's if published; otherwise develop a general view); geopolitical, economic and technological/sector environment; relevant actors (OCGs, state-nexus, hacktivists, insiders, supply-chain) with motivation/capability/campaign history; TTPs mapped to the latest MITRE ATT&CK.

Intelligence input — the questions collection must answer (ECB §3.2.4)

  • Target environment: what exposed information helps map the CIF-supporting network, locate CIFs, and find exploitable vulnerabilities? What infra has been targeted before? Which sector-critical vulnerabilities are likely present?
  • Social engineering: which roles/people could be targeted; what contact details & credentials are discoverable?
  • Third parties / MSPs: how do they change posture; which have been or could be compromised, and how would that enable entity compromise?

4 · Passive vs active collection — the hard rule

Passive (default)Active reconnaissance
WhatNo observable interaction with the target's live systems/people: OSINT, CT logs, scan-data archives, breach dumps, dark web, ethical HUMINTDirect, potentially observable interaction: scanning, probing, crafted contact, enumeration of the entity's assets
Who / whenTIP, throughout the TI phaseOnly the RTT, in the TI phase, at the TIP's request and after CT + TM approval
Rule—Must not alert the Blue Team or reveal that a TIBER test is underway

The TI phase must not tip off the Blue Team. Any step risking disclosure is prohibited unless gated through RTT + CT + TM. When in doubt, stay passive and record the gap as an intelligence limitation.

5 · Ethics, legality & OPSEC

  • OSINT = overt/public. HUMINT = overt or covert from human sources, always with documented ethical conduct and within the law; no entrapment, no unlawful access.
  • Handle leaked credentials/PII lawfully; never authenticate with discovered credentials during TI.
  • OPSEC: attributable infrastructure, codename discipline, need-to-know, encrypted comms/data rooms (per the project charter).

6 · Source grading & confidence

Record per source the reliability and the information's credibility (Admiralty/NATO A–F / 1–6), and give each analytic judgement a confidence level (high/medium/low) with reasoning — all in the evidence register, so selection is traceable, defensible and repeatable.

7 · Threat-actor assessment & profiling

  1. Categorise actor classes vs this entity.
  2. Name specific actors, with evidence-based reasoning on motivation/intent and why they'd target these CIFs/flags.
  3. Map actors → CIFs with the motivation per cell (ECB §3.3.3 pattern).
  4. Profile shortlisted actors on: Motivation · Goals/intent · Sophistication (script-kiddie→nation-state) · Agility (inflexible→adaptable) · Perseverance (opportunistic→motivated) · Purpose (meandering→direct).
  5. Threat level = capability × intent; take the highest-severity profiles into scenarios.
  6. Explain exclusions — why other classes were dropped.

8 · Scenario development

  • Longlist (≥6; ~6–10): high-level, reconnaissance-based, targeting every CIF in scope.
  • Shortlist (≥3), selected by the CTL on: TIP recommendation & threat-led nature · TM input · RTT feasibility · entity size/complexity/risk · breadth/depth across CIFs · (multi-party) ≥1 scenario covering the ICT third party.
  • Mandatory coverage: ≥1 each for availability, integrity, confidentiality; address authenticity where the flag does.
  • Scenario X: ≤1 non-threat-led / forward-looking (hybrid, novel TTPs, AI/quantum/deepfake); can be set in TI or converted during active testing with CT+TM+RTT agreement.
  • Backward vs forward: don't rely on history alone — forecast next moves, but keep evidence-based and hypothetical TTPs clearly distinct.

Scenario anatomy (per shortlisted scenario)

Name (actor + goal) · threat level (capability/intent) · end-to-end path across prepare → infiltrate → entrench → execute (RT's in → through → out) · mapping actor → objective/flag → ATT&CK tactic → technique → procedure · links to CIF, flag, impact dimension (C/I/A/authenticity), evidence, confidence.

9 · TTIR structure & report examples

No mandated format — any format is fine provided all ECB §2 / RTS Annex III content is present.

ECB §2 / RTS Annex III content spine

Scope of research · digital-footprint assessment · threat-landscape analysis (geopolitical/economic/technological) · threat profiles · ≥3 end-to-end scenarios (A/I/C) · optional Scenario X.

Norges TIBER-NO practical skeleton

  1. Executive summary
  2. Business overview from an intelligence perspective
  3. Intelligence on the entity's digital presence
  4. Threat actors — assessment → profiling
  5. High-level threat scenarios — threat level + attack flow with TTPs
  6. Appendices — TTP tables, OSINT raw data, scoring methodology

Worked example: see the fictional Meridian Bank TTIR and its scoping questionnaire.

10 · Passive OSINT tooling (indicative)

Attack surface / DNS: crt.sh & CT logs, Amass/subfinder, Shodan, Censys, FOFA. People/footprint: social media, email-pattern tools, Maltego, SpiderFoot. Breach/credential: HIBP, DeHashed, IntelX, infostealer-log feeds. Dark web / closed sources: vetted CTI feeds, marketplace monitoring. Always within the ethics/legal/OPSEC rules; prefer passive; record any active step as gated.
