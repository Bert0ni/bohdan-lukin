Finding — TEMPLATE

One card per finding. Findings are the atomic unit that rolls up into the RTTR (ECB Red Team Test Report Guidance, Jan 2025, §2 A3: "discovered vulnerabilities and other findings" — each with description + criticality, root-cause analysis, and a remediation recommendation with priority) and feeds the entity's Remediation Plan (Phase 6). Owner: RT / RTTM. Copy this block per finding; do not edit this blank.

A TIBER finding is not only a CVE/misconfiguration — it can be a control, process or people gap (e.g. "no alert fired on credential spraying for 4 h"). Rate by business impact, not by whether Domain Admin was reached. Keep real client data out of the repository.

Summary#

One or two sentences in plain language: what the weakness is and why it matters to the business.

Attack path / reproduction (ties to the RTTR storyline)#

Ordered steps the RTT took, split by in / through / out phase where relevant. Each step maps to a MITRE ATT&CK technique ID and cites execution evidence from the register.

  1. [Txxxx] step … — evidence EV-00X
  2. [Txxxx.yyy] step … — evidence EV-00Y

Evidence#

Reference the evidence register IDs (screenshots, logs, timestamps, command output). Do not paste raw client data here — point to the artifact store.

  • EV-00X — <what it proves>

Impact#

  • Business impact: effect on the CIF / service / data / regulatory exposure.
  • Technical impact: access, data, or control the attacker gained.
  • Realism / effort: skill, time and pre-requisites a real actor would need (was a leg-up used to reach this? note it — it changes the readiness conclusion).

Detection & response (input to Replay / Purple Teaming and the BTTR comparison)#

  • Detected? Yes / No / Partial — at which step, by which control.
  • Response: what the BT did, time-to-detect / time-to-respond, what was missed.

Root-cause analysis (RTTR §2 A3 — required for successful attacks)#

The underlying cause, not the symptom (e.g. "no egress filtering + flat network", "no MFA on the VPN", "EDR in detect-only mode"). Note contributing systemic/process factors.

Recommendation(s) & remediation priority (RTTR §2 A3 — required)#

  • Priority: P1 (immediate) / P2 (short-term) / P3 (planned).
  • Recommendation: root-cause-oriented, actionable, assigned-ownership-ready; short-term fix vs strategic resilience improvement where they differ.

References#

CWE / CVE / MITRE ATT&CK / vendor advisory / standard (OWASP, NIST SP 800-53, CIS) as applicable.

Criticality scale (define per engagement; keep it consistent across all findings)#

LevelMeaning (TIBER lens — impact to CIFs/resilience)
CriticalDirect, reliable compromise of a CIF or its data; systemic risk; immediate action.
HighSerious weakness enabling CIF compromise with modest effort, or a complete detection failure on a key path.
MediumMeaningful weakness; needs chaining or conditions; partial detection.
LowLimited impact or well-contained; hardening opportunity.
InformationalNo direct risk; observation, good-practice or resilience note.

Priority (P1–P3) answers "when to fix" and is distinct from criticality ("how bad"): a Medium finding on a systemically critical function can still warrant P1.
