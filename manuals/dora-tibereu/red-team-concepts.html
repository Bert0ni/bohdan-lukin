Red team concepts

Original overview of red teaming and where it sits among security-testing types, aligned to the TIBER-EU / DORA operating model. For detailed outside treatments see the further reading.

What red teaming is#

Red teaming is objective-driven adversary emulation: a trusted team reproduces the tactics, techniques and procedures (TTPs) of a realistic threat to test whether an organisation can prevent, detect and respond to it — end to end, across people, process and technology. Success is not "did we get Domain Admin"; it is what the defence saw and did against agreed objectives. In TIBER-EU this is formalised as flags tied to Critical or Important Functions (CIFs).

Engagement lifecycle (generic)#

flowchart LR A["Planning"] --> B["Reconnaissance"] B --> C["Get in: initial access"] C --> D["Stay in: persist and move"] D --> E["Act on objectives: reach flags"] E --> F["Reporting"] F --> G["Lessons learned: purple and remediation"]

In a TIBER-EU engagement this generic arc is governed by CT/TM and maps onto the formal phases — see the TIBER-EU lifecycle and phase guides.

Red teaming vs penetration testing vs vulnerability assessment#

Each manages a different kind of risk; they are complementary, not substitutes.

AspectVulnerability assessmentPenetration testRed team engagement
Primary goalEnumerate known weaknessesProve exploitability of specific flawsMeasure detection &amp; response against a realistic adversary
ScopeBroad, shallowDefined targets/systemsObjective-led, whole-org attack surface
FocusTechnologyTechnology (some process)People + process + technology
KnowledgeUsually credentialed/knownGrey/knownIntel-led, low prior knowledge
Defender awarenessAwareOften awareUnaware (blue team not told)
Threat modelKnown CVEs/misconfigReachable attack surfaceAdversary emulation (specific actor TTPs)
Typical durationHours–days~1–2 weeksWeeks–months (TIBER active phase ≥12 weeks)
Measure of valueCoverage / exposure reducedExploit impact demonstratedResilience: prevent / detect / respond outcomes

In TIBER-EU the comparison matters for scoping conversations: a TLPT is a red team engagement, not a pen test. Avoid promising specific compromises up front — scenarios are intelligence-derived later in the TTIR.

Team colours and the TIBER mapping#

mindmap root((Teams)) Red Adversary emulation TTPs and objectives TIBER role RT and RTTM Blue Detection and response SOC and IR Not informed during test Control Team Governs and deconflicts Risk and approvals TIBER role CT CTL and Test Manager Purple Red plus Blue collaboration Mandatory in TIBER closure
  • Red — the offensive team (our RT / RTTM).
  • Blue — the defenders (SOC/IR); in a TIBER test they are not told it is happening.
  • White → Control Team — governs, approves risk and deconflicts; in TIBER the CT/CTL with the authority-side Test Manager. (TIBER renamed White Team → Control Team in 2025.)
  • Purple — structured red+blue collaboration; mandatory in the TIBER closure phase.

Threat-led vs scenario-based#

  • Threat-led — scenarios derive from intelligence about actors that realistically target the entity (the TIBER default; ≥3 scenarios covering availability/integrity/confidentiality).
  • Scenario X — at most one forward-looking, non-threat-led scenario (novel/emerging TTPs).

See goal planning for turning objectives into flags, and threat-profile emulation for turning an actor into an emulation plan.
