Goal planning

Original guide to setting red-team objectives and turning them into testable flags, aligned to TIBER-EU. Planning quality determines whether a test produces learning or just activity.

Goals → objectives → flags#

flowchart TB G[Engagement goal<br/>business question] --> O[Objectives<br/>what to demonstrate] O --> F[Flags<br/>objective proof per CIF] F --> S[Success criteria<br/>what counts as reached] S --> M[Measures<br/>MoS / MoP]
  • Goal — the business question the entity wants answered (e.g. "Could an actor disrupt our instant-payments rail without us detecting it in time?").
  • Objective — the concrete thing the red team must demonstrate (reach a position, alter a process, access a dataset).
  • Flag — the agreed, evidenced proof an objective was met, tied to a specific CIF (defined in the SSD; refined in the TTIR and RTTP).
  • Success criteria — exactly what proves the flag (and what must not be touched to prove it).

Good objectives are CIF-anchored and safe-by-design#

PropertyWhy it matters
Tied to a CIFKeeps the test on systemically important function, not a trophy host
Impact-namedStates the aspect proven — confidentiality / integrity / availability / authenticity
EvidencedDefines the artefact that proves it (canary record, test token, demonstrated position)
BoundedSays how to prove impact without causing it (e.g. demonstrate, don't execute, a DoS)
Detection-relevantThe path to it exercises defences worth measuring

Measures: outcome vs performance#

  • Measures of Success (MoS) — were objectives/flags reached? (binary per flag, with evidence).
  • Measures of Performance (MoP) — how did the defence perform along the way? time-to-detect, time-to-respond, which controls fired, what was missed. MoP is where most learning lives and feeds Replay / Purple Teaming and the BTTR.

Success is measured against agreed objectives and defender detection/response — never "Domain Admin". A flag reached via an approved, documented leg-up still validates the later defences; an undocumented shortcut does not. See safety &amp; risk.

Planning checklist (condensed)#

  • Each CIF in scope has at least one candidate objective.
  • Objectives cover availability, integrity and confidentiality across the scenario set.
  • Every flag has a written success criterion and a "prove-without-causing-impact" method.
  • Learning goals from the entity are captured and reflected in scenario selection.
  • Leg-up triggers and deadlines are pre-agreed (so a blocked path still yields learning).
